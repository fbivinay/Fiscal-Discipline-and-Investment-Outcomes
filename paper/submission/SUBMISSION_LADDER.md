# Submission ladder

Ordered by speed, which is the stated priority. Every venue here is legitimate
and peer-reviewed.

## The rule that governs everything

**One journal at a time.** Simultaneous submission is a serious ethics breach in
economics, and Economics Bulletin's terms state the manuscript must not be under
consideration elsewhere. Their terms also make it the author's responsibility to
keep editors informed about related submissions and publication plans while a
paper is under review.

Descend this ladder only as each venue rejects. Two months buys one to two
attempts, not ten.

## Ruled out, and why

- **Oxford Bulletin of Economics and Statistics** — publishes applied econometrics
  with large panels and credible identification. N = 50 with ten clusters and a
  confounded headline result would be desk-rejected.
- **Economic Theory Bulletin** (Springer) — a pure theory journal despite the
  similar name. This paper is empirical with no theoretical contribution. Out of
  scope.
- **IJPREMS, IJARIIT** — advertise publication in 1–4 hours and 24 hours
  respectively. That is the absence of peer review, not fast peer review. IJARIIT
  appears on the stop-predatory-journals database. Both lean on "UGC CARE listed",
  a credential UGC discontinued on 3 October 2024 and stopped maintaining in
  February 2025.
- **JEFMS** — "one week from final acceptance" is production time and not itself a
  red flag, but the venue was not verified either way. Check DOAJ, the editorial
  board, and the stated *review* time before considering it.

## What raises the ceiling

The binding constraint is not venue choice. It is N = 50, ten clusters, and a
headline result the paper itself shows is absorbed by region. Aiming higher costs
months and yields rejections without changing the paper.

The conclusion already names the fix: a twenty-six state cross-section, whose
fiscal side is already in the FC-16 annexures. That paper, with an identification
strategy that works, is the one to send to a stronger journal.
